# Personal Reflections on Learning Painting Design, Computer Programming, Violin, and Other Fields

- The Internet has broken down cognitive barriers, allowing things that people in the Middle Ages could never have imagined to happen today. What? Do you think this is unfair to people across different historical periods? Haha, don't overthink it. After all, in some aspects, people in every era have their own crises.

- Different crises, different chains.

    - From a macro perspective of oneself, our crises are only physical perception, social interests, and self-closure. However, the changes of the times have added some difficulties to us — information illusion and the training of rational tools.

    - In this era, the amount of information is growing exponentially. It can almost be considered a kind of multi-threaded development — a flourishing of many possibilities.

      Although information has increased, garbage has also increased. I firmly believe that high-quality information is like the noble ideals of those few strangers I have never met... Without proper handling, it will inevitably be contaminated by utilitarianism and obedience.

        - Therefore, there are some questions that need to be answered:

        - 1. How can we enhance our own stability?

        - 2. How can we filter and integrate high-quality resources that fit ourselves?

    - Think about it. This inevitably belongs to you, hahaha!

- If this is the case, then merely relying on our innate curiosity, who knows how many Renaissance-style polymaths would appear in the world? I am only half-joking.

    - First, I will roughly divide human knowledge into several categories:

        - Natural Sciences
        - Social Sciences
        - Humanities
        - Formal Sciences
        - Engineering Sciences

      The smaller fields within each major domain are interconnected and generate new fields. This is also the key entry point for mastering rapid learning.

    - In my previous article, I discussed knowledge related to isomorphism. Now, if I separate the problems I encounter into individual disciplines and examine them independently, I estimate that they would present a clear linear developmental trend:

        - Basic skills (initial construction of neural circuits)
        - Intermediate stage (forming low-cost models and myelination)
        - Advanced stage (building broad neural pathways)

      However, once we simplify and process the models of a field (mapping, projection, etc.) and transfer them into other fields, you will discover that other knowledge is actually not so different (the wonderful application of meta-structures).

      Therefore, what matters more is not understanding itself, but how to inform the body — through reasonable deliberate practice, forming muscle memory.

    - This is also the fundamental reason why I mention violin, painting, and other practices.

        - During my zero-based painting practice challenge, I absorbed a large amount of theoretical knowledge (form, color, position). This gave me an extremely strong sense of confidence and security, causing me to neglect training and external feedback (which is also the only drawback of self-learning).

          The result was that my professional knowledge was so widely understood that even "real professionals" could hardly find problems with it... Yet my actual achievements were far from sufficient... A pity, isn't it?

        - Violin is even more so. It requires the mobilization of more senses. Your vision, fingers, palms, shoulders, arms, and even your entire body are involved. Your ears must be used to the extreme.

          The control of both hands is especially interesting (I discovered that the left and right hands are not actually used together in the way we imagine!!!). I think this is what true embodied experience is...

- Work in reality is merely a label used for positioning. Just like disciplines, it does not need to be divided so clearly...

  What matters is whether you can discover your own "habits" within your work.

  You see, doctors and teachers are equivalent, hackers and psychologists are equivalent, mathematicians and artists are equivalent, network engineering and law are equivalent...
